Tang Biophotonic Imaging Lab · CURE Fellowship

Optical imaging for liver transplantation

Can high-resolution optical imaging help characterize bile duct tissue before liver transplantation?

Bile duct injury can complicate liver transplantation. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) captures cross-sectional images of tissue, giving the research team a way to study microstructural changes before comparing them with reference methods.

My contribution

I processed donor bile duct OCT scans, annotated tissue boundaries, and extracted image features. I standardized acquisition metadata, compared adjacent scans, and flagged ambiguous boundaries for review.

Outcome and status

The team studied 20 donor bile ducts ex vivo and compared OCT findings with confocal microscopy and histology. I presented my work at the CURE Summer Research Symposium and the OU UReCA Showcase. The team’s conference abstract appeared in the 2025 END2CANCER program.

Limitations

The study evaluated tissue ex vivo. A transplant viability decision rule would require prospective clinical validation.

INQUIRE Lab · Provost’s Summer UReCA Fellowship

Model quantization for portable imaging

How does model compression change the trade-off between accuracy, inference time, and file size on a CPU?

For resource-constrained imaging systems, a smaller model can be attractive. Image classification on a held-out public microscopy dataset provided a controlled setting to measure how compression affected the full set of deployment metrics.

My contribution

I built a reusable Python preprocessing pipeline for public NIH/NLM malaria cell images and benchmarked two TensorFlow Lite classifier variants. I held the split fixed, checked for file-path overlap, and used confusion matrices to examine errors.

Outcome and status

I prepared the 2026 UReCA fellowship poster on quantization for portable biomedical imaging. The benchmark measured classification accuracy, model footprint, and inference time under matched conditions.

Limitations

This was a dataset and CPU benchmark. Deployment latency, power use, and clinical performance would require additional testing.

Methods & tools

  • Python
  • TensorFlow Lite
  • Class-stratified splits
  • Single-thread CPU benchmarking

INQUIRE Lab · Undergraduate research

Controlled evaluation of edge inference

How can matched baselines and realistic evaluation splits isolate where a model architecture helps?

When evaluating spiking architectures, preprocessing choices and differences in decision rules can obscure which component affects performance. An ECG experiment poses a related challenge because samples from the same patient can be correlated.

My contribution

I led the design and evaluation of a spoken-action benchmark with matched controls, applying calibration, risk–coverage analysis, and paired resampling. I also contributed to ECG edge-inference research using patient-grouped recordings and simulated inference deadlines.

Outcome and status

The spoken-action and ECG work are research projects. My published IEEE coauthorship is a separate comparative study of neuromorphic hardware for edge intelligence.

Limitations

These evaluations address specific datasets and timing assumptions. Performance in prospective or hardware-in-the-loop settings requires separate evaluation.

Methods & tools

  • Matched baselines
  • Calibration
  • Paired resampling
  • Patient-grouped evaluation
