Peribiliary glands & cystic dilation

A source-linked overview of gland anatomy, interpretation of cystic dilation, and open research questions.

Peribiliary glands and cysts

Peribiliary glands sit in and around the walls of larger bile ducts. Cysts of peribiliary gland origin commonly lie beside bile ducts, including near the hepatic hilum. The published literature includes extrahepatic as well as intrahepatic lesions.

  • Bazerbachi et al. · Systematic review (2018)
What counts as dilation?

Bosselmann and colleagues used 2 mm to distinguish ectasia from cysts in their study of end-stage alcohol-related liver disease. Their cutoff describes that study’s histologic classification. A systematic review documented a wider range of cyst sizes across the included studies.

  • Bosselmann et al. · Histopathology study (2020)
What imaging can show

Cysts can appear as a series of small fluid-filled structures alongside the ducts. Their position and lack of visible communication with the biliary lumen help distinguish them from duct dilation. An ex vivo OCT study investigated tissue microstructure at a different scale from conventional imaging.

  • Bazerbachi et al. · Systematic review (2018)
Clinical context

Peribiliary cysts are often incidental. The systematic review describes cases associated with underlying liver disease, and a smaller subset with obstruction or diagnostic confusion. Case reports can overrepresent unusual or symptomatic presentations.

  • Bazerbachi et al. · Systematic review (2018)
Dilation versus mimics

A cyst adjacent to a bile duct, a dilated bile duct, and a lesion arising inside a duct are different structures. Anatomic location and communication with the duct help frame the differential. A single size measurement provides limited information.

  • Bazerbachi et al. · Systematic review (2018)
Open research questions

Longitudinal data and consistent definitions would improve comparisons across populations. For OCT studies, useful next steps include testing reproducibility, quantifying agreement with histology, and evaluating performance in the intended setting.

  • Bazerbachi et al. · Systematic review (2018)
