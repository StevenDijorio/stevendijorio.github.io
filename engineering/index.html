INQUIRE Lab · ART InTRO Fellowship

Analog spiking-neuron hardware

Design, simulation, and bench testing of a Zener-clamped analog spiking-neuron circuit.

Analog spiking circuits offer a hardware path for event-based computation. My work on the FUGAZ circuit studied how measured firing behavior compares with simulation and supported a fellowship proposal for low-power neuroprosthetic applications.

My contribution

I designed and assembled the circuit board, bench-tested it with an oscilloscope and function generator, and compared the measured waveforms with NGSpice and COMSOL simulations. I analyzed spike trains in Python and MATLAB.

Outcome and status

I used bench and simulation comparisons to evaluate the prototype and developed an ART InTRO proposal to adapt the design for low-power neuroprosthetics.

Limitations

The circuit is research hardware. The proposed application would require device-level testing and validation.

University of Oklahoma · Biomedical instrumentation

Wearable stretch-sensor prototype

A wearable sensing prototype for exploring limb circumference monitoring in lymphedema.

The team explored using a stretch sensor to detect changes in circumference, which required conditioning a small resistance change for readout on a microcontroller.

My contribution

I built the team’s Wheatstone-bridge sensor circuit and used Arduino Uno serial voltage readings to troubleshoot bridge imbalance.

Outcome and status

Serial readouts provided a way to inspect the electrical response and investigate imbalance as the team developed the prototype.

Limitations

The work was a course prototype. Sensor calibration and clinical measurement accuracy would require further validation.

Methods & tools

  • Wheatstone bridge
  • Stretch sensing
  • Arduino Uno
  • Serial voltage measurements

University of Oklahoma · Senior design

Adaptive toothbrush holder

Requirements, concept selection, and prototype fit checks for an assistive oral-care device.

The capstone team explored an adaptive toothbrush holder, with design decisions shaped by fit, adjustability, and ease of use.

My contribution

I documented requirements, compared three concepts with a weighted decision matrix, and used fit checks to inform a crown-strap revision. I applied standards knowledge while reviewing design choices.

Outcome and status

The team iterated the prototype following fit checks. The revised strap addressed an issue identified during that process.

Limitations

Fit checks informed development. Formal device verification, compliance testing, and user validation would be separate steps.
