Biliary injury in liver transplantation

Six short, source-linked topics on gland injury, transplant complications, and how researchers study them.

Peribiliary glands and their blood supply

The peribiliary vascular plexus supplies the bile duct wall. A donor-biopsy study found that injury to deeper peribiliary glands and the surrounding vascular plexus was more common in grafts that later developed non-anastomotic strictures.

Ischemia–reperfusion injury

Histologic injury to donor bile ducts before implantation provides a way to study later biliary complications. The association between deeper gland injury and subsequent strictures makes the gland compartment a target for transplant research.

Allograft rejection

The Banff Working Group describes patterns of bile duct injury in liver allograft rejection and considers alternative explanations for the findings. This raises a research question about how to distinguish overlapping injury patterns.

Cytomegalovirus and biliary complications

Observational studies have reported differing associations between cytomegalovirus infection and biliary complications after transplantation. One retrospective study did not find an independent association with non-anastomotic strictures. Differences in definitions and cohorts limit comparison.

Non-anastomotic biliary strictures

These strictures occur away from the surgical connection. Donor-biopsy work links them to gland and microvascular injury, supporting further study of how the bile duct epithelium repairs after transplantation.

Comparing evidence on biliary injury

Donor-biopsy studies, rejection criteria, and imaging studies answer different questions. When comparing findings, consider the sampled tissue, how the outcome was defined, and whether the measurements were made ex vivo or in the intended clinical setting.
